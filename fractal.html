Fractal cache

Recompute avoidance you can watch.

Two-phase cache, modeled on the route-visualization pipeline I work on. Phase 1 (expensive) computes the escape-time iterations per pixel and caches them by (zoom, x, y, iter). Phase 2 (cheap) applies a color palette to those cached iterations. Panning back over seen ground is a cache hit — and switching the palette recolors from cached iterations with no recompute at all. Expensive base cached once, cheap metadata layer on top: more meaningful work, not just faster work — spend compute on the base, refuse to redo it for a cheap change. Click to zoom, drag to pan, switch palettes to watch the recolor cost nothing.

The overlay badges each tile with its cache state: blue = computed (with the ms it took), green = recolored (phase 2 only), and grey = served from cache (no work). Pan around, then pan back — the tiles you've seen turn grey: that's the recompute being avoided. Switch palettes and they all go green (recolor, no recompute). Compare cold vs cached renders the view with an empty cache, then again from cache, and shows the compute the cache saved. Shared across the pooled agents; live only while my desktop is on.
