Work/Life Balance

I care about excelling at whatever I'm doing, whether that's engineering good systems, swimming in a past life, or being a good friend, teammate, and partner.

The way I've come to see it, doing any of that well means being well-rounded across disciplines. What you carry from one scope into another is where diversity of ideas comes from, and that mix is usually what cracks the hard problems. Staying in a single lane makes you narrow, not deep.

I've always had a lot going on outside of code, and I'm not interested in the version of this career where that gets stripped away. The stretches where the rest of my life is in good shape are the same ones where I do my sharpest work. That's not a coincidence, but it's also not the reason I do it.

Part of it is a sustainable rhythm. I do my best work in one or two focused blocks a day and then stop, instead of trying to be on for twelve hours. A lot of that habit comes from swimming. I swam Division I at Notre Dame, and competitive swimming is really just years of showing up every morning and doing the work whether you feel like it or not. That carried straight over: consistency beats intensity, and rest is part of the training, not a break from it.

A few things keep me level. Swimming and yoga are how I stay in my body and get out of my head. Guitar and drums are the things I've done longest outside of work. I've played guitar for over ten years and I record at home, and drumming is where I go when I need to stop thinking about a problem for a while. My girlfriend and I have two cats, and honestly a slow morning with coffee and the cats does more for my focus than another hour at the desk would. I read a fair amount, and I try to keep learning things that have nothing to do with work.

A lot of this traces back to doing the full IB diploma in high school. IB drills exactly this idea: you're meant to be a universal learner, not someone who confines himself to one discipline and calls that well-rounded. It stuck.

So I don't think of swimming, music, or a slow morning with the cats as time off from the work. It's where a lot of my range and better ideas actually come from. And either way, I'm going to keep being a whole person, not just an engineer. The work has always been better for it, but that's a byproduct, not the point.
