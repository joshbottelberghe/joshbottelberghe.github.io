Work/Life Balance

I take my work seriously. I've never thought that meant letting it become the whole of who I am.

I've always had a lot going on outside of code, and I'm not interested in the version of this career where that gets stripped away. I've never had to choose, either. The stretches where the rest of my life was in good shape are the same ones where I did my sharpest work. That's not a coincidence, but it's also not the reason I do it.

The core of it is a sustainable rhythm. I do my best work in one or two focused blocks a day and then stop, instead of trying to be on for twelve hours. A lot of that habit comes from swimming. I swam Division I at Notre Dame, and competitive swimming is really just years of showing up every morning and doing the work whether you feel like it or not. That carried straight over: consistency beats intensity, and rest is part of the training, not a break from it.

A few things keep me level. Swimming and yoga are how I stay in my body and get out of my head. Guitar and drums are the things I've done longest outside of work. I've played guitar for over ten years and I record at home, and drumming is where I go when I need to stop thinking about a problem for a while. My girlfriend and I have two cats, and honestly a slow morning with coffee and the cats does more for my focus than another hour at the desk would. I read a fair amount, and I try to keep learning things that have nothing to do with work.

A lot of how I think about this comes from doing the full IB diploma in high school. IB pushes you to be a universal learner. The idea is that you can't confine yourself to code, or to any one discipline, and still call yourself well-learned or balanced. And it isn't really about the time away from the desk for its own sake. It's that what you carry back into your own domain from everything outside it is where range comes from, and range is what lets you beat genuinely hard problems. Swimming, music, reading, all of it feeds the engineering more than another hour at the keyboard would.

So I don't think of swimming, music, or a slow morning with the cats as time off from the work. It's where a lot of my range and better ideas actually come from. And either way, I'm going to keep being a whole person, not just an engineer. The work has always been better for it, but that's a byproduct, not the point.
