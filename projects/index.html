Work & Projects

Most of what I've built lives inside one company, so this is more a description of the work than links to code.

Working at a startup, you end up owning a lot. Over the last few years I've gone from shipping single features to owning a core pipeline, a node-side subsystem, the team's route-intelligence tooling, and the CI across every repo. A few of the bigger pieces:

  • Route-visualization pipeline · the system I own

    It takes the live routing state off a fleet of nodes across three clouds, stores it, computes per-cloud-region and global views of what's reachable and how, including segmentation and metadata routes, and serves that to customer APIs. Hundreds of customer networks, tens of thousands of routes each, a freshness target under five minutes, and no major outages while I've owned it. I've rebuilt the compute engine three times as it grew.

    Design note: recompute avoidance
  • Partial compute · route-viz scaling · patent

    The rebuild I'm most proud of. Instead of recomputing a network's whole view on every change, the engine now does work in proportion to what actually changed. On a single-segment update that's a 99.6% cache hit and about 768 MB of memory instead of 12 GB, and roughly a 3x scaling improvement overall. It's one of the two patents that came out of my route-viz work.

    Read how it works
  • Route dampening & suppression · node-side system

    A system that puts a penalty and a half-life on routes that flap, so churn doesn't overwhelm everything downstream. I wrote the algorithm, tuned the production parameters from real traffic analysis, and added metrics for it. Later I caught a regression in it and got the revert out the same day it showed up.

  • Selector Stats · traffic observability

    I picked this up end to end when a teammate went on leave, in a part of the system I hadn't worked in before. I did the design, the API, and the rollout, then root-caused a wrong number down to a bad metric on another team's side and drove the fix through them. Shipped it behind a flag and validated it live on call.

  • route-detective · Go · route-intelligence tooling

    A Go server that makes our historical route archive queryable, including by an AI agent. I rewrote it into a single self-contained binary with 18 tools and took it from v1 to v2.1 in about two weeks. The team picked it up for on-call, upgrade checks, and tracking down route churn.

  • CI/CD & security across the fleet · 18 Go repos

    I brought consistent CI, dependency and secret scanning, and merge gates to all 18 of our routing Go repos, plus a weekly health audit. Along the way I fixed real CVEs, an access-control bug from a security probe, and cleaned a lot of hardcoded credentials out of the fleet. The point was to raise the floor for everyone, not just my own changes.

  • Working across teams · the connective work

    A lot of my work crosses team lines. Something breaks in one service but starts in another, and I trace it and drive the fix through whoever owns it. Day to day that's working with Product, QA, and the other routing teams to actually get things shipped, and mentoring a junior engineer along the way.
