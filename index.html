Josh Bottelberghe

Software engineer working on networking, distributed systems, and infrastructure at scale.

Hi, I'm Josh. I'm a software engineer in Vancouver, Washington, and I live with my girlfriend and our two cats. I've been into software since I was a kid, and I swam competitively the whole way through, Division I at Notre Dame, until the software career took over from the swimming one. A lot of how I work still comes from swimming. It taught me rigor, the kind where you show up every day and do the work even when it's boring, and a real belief that steady effort compounds into improvement. It's why I don't flinch at a daunting problem so much as ask how to make it approachable and start chipping at it. Outside of work I play guitar and drums and record at home, cook most nights on cast iron, and put too many hours into Factorio.

What keeps me interested is building software that actually makes a difference: something that makes a person's work easier or solves a real problem for them. I like it simple and solid, and a lot of the time getting there means cutting the work rather than doing it faster. I also care about who I'm doing it with. The best work I've done has come out of egoless teams that genuinely want to learn and get better, all pointed at some thorny problem everyone is trying to crack together.

The rest of this site is the actual work: what I've built, how I think about it, and a few writeups that go deeper.

Most of my work is on network control-plane systems: the ones that take the live routing state off a fleet of nodes and turn it into something operators and customers can actually reason about. Day to day that's Go, BGP and FRR internals, and the usual at-scale stuff, Kubernetes, AWS, Redis, S3. The part I like most is where networking meets distributed systems, keeping a pipeline fast and correct when the naive approach costs you more as the whole system grows, not just as the changes do.
